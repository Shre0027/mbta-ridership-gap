Two official numbers. One Blue Line.

The MBTA publishes two ridership datasets that disagree by up to 23%. The MBTA suspected growth in East Boston. A station-by-station check points downtown.

01 · The problem

Same line, same season, two different answers.

The MBTA's Office of Performance Management & Innovation (OPMI) publishes both of these. Both are "official." They are built in different ways, for different jobs.

In February 2025 a developer asked on the MBTA's public forum why Fall 2023 Blue Line numbers differed by 23.4%. OPMI replied that the methods differ, named East Boston development as the likely cause, and said aligning the two is ongoing work.

So what?This isn't a one-season glitch. The two datasets follow different rules. On the Blue Line, those rules always undercount in the same direction, so something structural is going on.

02 · The station test

Where on the Blue Line does the gap come from?

Dataset A is published only as one number per line. But it is built from fare-gate taps, and the MBTA publishes those taps by station. So I compared, station by station, the raw taps that feed Dataset A with the boardings Dataset B models.

So what?In Fall 2025, regular stations show the model 14–30% above the taps, the normal allowance for riders who don't tap. At State and Government Center it counts 13–21 times more. Riders switching from the Orange or Green Line board the Blue Line there without ever tapping again.

03 · Over time

Two quiet method changes made the gap wider.

Running the same test for every season from 2017 to 2025 turns up two shifts in how riders are counted. Neither one is flagged in the data.

So what?The count fell about 90% between 2018 and 2019. Riders didn't vanish. The most likely cause is a new survey split factor. It now credits Blue with only about 10% of entries.

So what?Before COVID the model matched the taps almost exactly. Since 2023 it adds 16–24% for riders who don't tap, likely using faregate passenger counters.

So why do the two numbers disagree?

Behind-the-gate transfers downtown

About a third of Blue Line boardings happen at State and Government Center, mostly by riders who already tapped in on another line. The tap-based method has to estimate them with fixed factors. The trip model follows each trip.

A split-factor change around 2019

Taps credited to the Blue Line at Government Center fell about 90% from one year to the next. That points to a change in the survey split, and the dataset doesn't flag it.

A bigger allowance for non-tappers after COVID

The model's uplift at ordinary stations rose from about 1.05× to about 1.2×. A single fixed factor in the monthly method would not move with it.

And East Boston? Maverick, Airport, Wood Island, Orient Heights and the Revere stations scale steadily, at almost the same ratio as every other regular station. The data doesn't show them driving the gap.

Caveat: OPMI doesn't publish the exact factors behind Dataset A, so how much of the gap each reason explains is inferred from the published data, not confirmed by the MBTA.

04 · Recommendations

What I'd bring to the data owners.

Written the way I'd hand it to a product owner: specific, cheap to act on, and tied to the evidence.

Document

Publish the scaling factors and their history

List the non-interaction, station-split and transfer factors used in Dataset A, with the date each one changed. Users could then reconcile the datasets themselves.

Flag

Mark method changes in the data

Add a method-version field or a change note to every dataset, so a 90% drop at one station isn't mistaken for a real ridership change.

Standardize

One name and ID per station and line

"State" vs "State Street," and a Green Line split into four branches in only one season. Shared IDs would make every join reliable.

Reconcile

Ship a published bridge table

For each line and season: taps, plus each factor's contribution, equals Dataset A. Next to it, Dataset B. The gap stops being a mystery and becomes a line item.

05 · Method

How this was built

  • Sources: MBTA Monthly Ridership by Mode and Line; Rail Ridership by Season, Route/Line and Stop (Fall 2017–2019, 2023, 2024, 2025); Gated Station Entries (historical). All from the MBTA Open Data Portal.
  • Window: weekdays, September to December. Monthly figures are day-weighted, which reproduces the forum post's Fall 2023 numbers exactly.
  • Stop-level totals: the sum of average weekday boardings across every stop, direction and time period.
  • Station test: raw gate taps averaged over non-holiday weekdays, compared with modeled boardings at each station.
  • Data quality fixes: "State Street" mapped to "State"; Fall 2025 Green Line branches rolled up to one line. Six issues are logged in the repo.
